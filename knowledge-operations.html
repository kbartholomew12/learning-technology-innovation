CASE STUDY 03 · KNOWLEDGE SUPPORT & WORKFLOW REDESIGN

Making Information and Processes Easier to Use

Two related improvements inside the Microsoft Teams environment: a more complete faculty onboarding guide, and redesigned operational workflows that improve communication, coordination, and follow-through across the School of Science.

Better systems reduce the amount of effort people spend finding information, tracking details, and correcting avoidable errors. MY APPROACH TO OPERATIONAL IMPROVEMENT

Two Connected Improvements

These projects solve different problems. One improves how faculty access information. The other improves how leaders coordinate work and manage complex processes.

01

Faculty Onboarding Guide

I adapted the college's existing onboarding document into a more complete, inclusive mini-manual that brings together the information new faculty actually need, with direct links to relevant resources.

02

Operational Workflow Redesign

I created clearer Teams-based workflows for communication among deans, faculty management, and class observations, and I am currently building an automated workflow for a complex scheduling and instructional-materials process.

Faculty Onboarding Guide
From a basic document to a practical mini-manual

Valencia already had an onboarding document. Rather than replace it, I used it as the foundation and expanded it to include the broader set of information faculty need to begin working effectively.

The revised guide functions like a mini-manual: it brings key expectations, practical guidance, and direct links to supporting resources into one place so new faculty do not have to piece together critical information from multiple sources.

The emphasis was usability and completeness—making the existing process more useful rather than creating a new system simply for the sake of change.

Operational Workflow Redesign
Improving coordination inside a complex academic operation

A separate need involved how deans communicate and coordinate faculty-management work across the School of Science. I created clearer Teams-based workflows to support department communication, faculty management, and class observations.

These workflows create more visible, shared processes for work that can otherwise become fragmented across emails, individual tracking systems, and informal follow-up.

I am also currently developing a workflow to automate a particularly complicated process involving faculty schedules and instructional-material information—work that traditionally requires multiple emails, repeated tracking, and follow-up.

The Automation in Progress

The current project focuses on a high-friction process where schedule confirmation and instructional-material information must be collected accurately from multiple faculty members and then tracked through follow-up.

01

Start with Existing Data

Use faculty schedule information already maintained by the school rather than recreating it manually.

02

Collect Structured Responses

Give faculty a clear way to confirm assignments, identify needed changes, and provide required course information.

03

Route & Track

Reduce dependence on separate emails and manual follow-up by moving responses into a shared workflow.

04

Reduce Downstream Errors

Improve the accuracy of schedule and instructional-material information before errors affect students, budgets, or administrative workload.
