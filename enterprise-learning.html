01 · Scalable Learning Architecture

Building consistency without losing flexibility

I helped build the learning and operating infrastructure that supported the growth of Nutrition from 4 sections on one campus to more than 70 sections across five campuses.

The model connected standardized digital course architecture, aligned outcomes and assessments, reusable learning resources, faculty onboarding, cross-campus planning, quality expectations, and ongoing improvement.

The goal was not to make every instructor identical. It was to create a strong common foundation so learners received a consistent, high-quality experience while faculty retained appropriate professional flexibility.

02 · Learning Governance & Quality

Turning quality principles into a usable governance model

Scalable learning requires more than strong content. It also requires shared expectations for design, accessibility, implementation, evaluation, and improvement.

I helped develop an institution-specific online-learning framework connecting course design standards, observable instructor practices, accessibility, peer and dean review, faculty development, innovation, and continuous improvement.

My approach to governance is practical: establish enough structure to protect quality and consistency while keeping systems usable for the people expected to work within them.

03 · Workforce Enablement & Performance Support

Making information and workflows easier to use

Learning does not always need to be a course. Sometimes the stronger solution is better onboarding, clearer information, a more usable workflow, or support available when someone actually needs it.

I redesigned faculty onboarding resources and created Microsoft Teams-based workflows that support communication, faculty management, class observations, scheduling, and instructional-material processes across a complex academic operation.

The goal is to reduce friction: make information easier to find, responsibilities clearer, and recurring processes more reliable.

04 · AI-Enabled Learning & Emerging Technology

Using emerging technology where it improves learning

I approach emerging technology with a simple question: does it solve a meaningful learning problem?

My recent work includes AI-supported debate and critique, individualized inquiry experiences, error-analysis activities, interactive simulations, browser-based science labs, and faculty-facing models for responsible AI adoption.

The technology is not the outcome. Each example begins with the learning goal and uses technology only where it strengthens reasoning, feedback, practice, engagement, or scalability.

How I Approach Enterprise Learning

Learning strategy starts with the conditions people need to succeed.

⌘

Design for Scale

Build reusable systems that can expand across people, locations, and delivery models.

◎

Start with the Need

Determine whether the right solution is learning, information, workflow, technology, coaching, or a combination.

⚖

Balance Standards & Judgment

Create enough governance to protect quality without designing systems people cannot realistically use.

↗

Support Adoption

Pair new systems and technology with guidance, learning, communication, and support.

◔

Measure What Changes

Use performance information, learning outcomes, and feedback to improve the system over time.

✦

Use Technology Intentionally

Adopt technology because it improves learning or performance, not because the technology itself is new.

I believe people can learn when the right conditions are in place.

The strongest learning systems create those conditions.

For me, that means designing experiences and systems that are clear, accessible, practical, and connected to the work people actually need to do.

The solution may be a course, a resource, a workflow, a platform, a coaching structure, or a combination of those things. The goal is not simply to deliver content. It is to help people learn, apply what they know, and perform more effectively.
