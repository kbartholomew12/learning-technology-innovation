What the Quality Framework Looks Like in Practice

Selected examples from Valencia College's Rubric for Online Competencies: Criteria for Quality Course Design and Delivery. The framework connects what is built into an online course with the observable instructor actions that support the student learning experience.

The Governance Model

The rubric was designed as more than a checklist. It connects standards, observable practice, supporting resources, and continuous improvement.

01

Design Criteria

What should be clearly present in the online course environment.

02

Delivery Criteria

Observable instructor actions that support and activate the course design.

03

Annotations & Resources

Context, examples, institutional resources, and alignment to educator competencies.

04

Continuous Improvement

Peer and dean review used as a developmental process to strengthen online learning.

Five Areas of Online Learning Quality

Each area combines course design expectations with instructor delivery practices.

🚦

Start Right

The opening experience establishes clarity, expectations, access to resources, and a clear path into the course.

  • Dedicated orientation module and obvious starting point
  • Easy-to-find syllabus and full-term schedule
  • Navigation and course-structure guidance
  • Course expectations, communication protocols, and grading policies
  • Technology requirements and technical support
  • Academic integrity, accommodations, support services, attendance verification, and community-building activities
♿

Accessibility & Usability

Accessibility is treated as part of course quality rather than as a separate compliance layer.

  • Clear and consistent course organization
  • Keyboard-accessible navigation
  • Alternative access to course materials
  • Accurate descriptions for non-text elements
  • Consistent color, text, layout, and accessibility considerations
  • Captioned video and transcripts for audio
🎯

Learning Outcomes, Activities & Materials

Course elements are expected to connect clearly to measurable learning outcomes and meaningful student work.

  • Measurable module outcomes aligned to course outcomes
  • Clearly stated learning goals
  • Content organized into relevant, manageable learning segments
  • Critical-thinking opportunities
  • Varied assessments sequenced to support growth
  • Instructional activities and materials aligned to intended outcomes
🖥

Course Technology & Support

Technology is evaluated by whether it supports learning and active engagement— not by novelty.

  • Technology supports course learning outcomes
  • Tools contribute to active learning and engagement
  • Course links are functional and accessible
  • Privacy-policy information is available for required external technologies
  • Technology use is supported by clear guidance and relevant resources
💬

Engaged Teaching

Quality online learning includes what the instructor does during the course, not only how the course shell is designed.

  • Clear communication and response expectations
  • Defined feedback practices
  • Current gradebook and constructive feedback
  • Formative feedback throughout the learning process
  • Module overviews connecting outcomes, content, activities, and assessment
  • Active-learning interaction and clear participation expectations
Governance did not mean standardizing innovation away.

Each major section of the rubric included an “Acknowledgement of Outstanding Online Innovation and Quality” area where reviewers could recognize course elements that created an especially strong or innovative student-learning experience. That design choice allowed the framework to establish a quality baseline while still valuing creativity and professional judgment.
