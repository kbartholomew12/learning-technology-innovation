01 · Responsible AI & Assessment

Designing assessments that work in the age of AI

Challenge: Generic AI-generated answers can conceal whether students can interpret data, reason scientifically, and recognize errors.

My contribution: Co-presented AI in Science Education: Designing Assessments That Work with Jeremy Rodriguez, translating authentic assessment, AI literacy, academic integrity, and discipline-specific redesign into strategies for science faculty.

Leadership relevance: Faculty guidance that emphasizes stronger evidence of learning over unreliable detection alone.

Methods and scope

Examples included data interpretation, error critique, metacognition, student-generated observations, authentic scenarios, and transparent expectations for AI use in biology, chemistry, physics, environmental science, anatomy, and nutrition. These are faculty-development examples, not claims of outcomes from institution-wide deployment.

02 · Outcomes & Quality Assurance

BSC1005: making an OER transition academically rigorous

Challenge: Replace an established textbook without losing alignment to state-mandated learning outcomes or Valencia's evidences of learning.

My contribution: Crosswalked the previous textbook and selected OER, then aligned learning content and formative assessments with summative measures of the required evidences of learning.

Leadership relevance: A repeatable approach to curriculum modernization grounded in assessment validity—not tool novelty.

Evidence planned

A permission-cleared, redacted crosswalk excerpt can show the connections from learning outcomes to readings, student practice, and assessment evidence. No confidential curriculum files are embedded in this page.

06 · Scale & Leadership

Scaling instruction without losing quality

Challenge: Grow access to Nutrition instruction across campuses with reliable faculty support and learning outcomes.

My contribution: Integrated curriculum design, standardized course shells, faculty onboarding and mentoring, enrollment planning, and continuous improvement.

Measured result: Expanded from four sections on one campus to more than 70 across five campuses; introductory-course success moved from 67% in its first term to 83–91% across modalities.

My approach to academic AI

Start with the learning outcome and define what students must demonstrate. Determine whether AI supports inquiry, formative feedback, accessibility, or faculty productivity. Establish appropriate boundaries and transparent expectations; consider privacy, bias, accuracy, and accessibility. Preserve human judgment. Assess the educational value and improve the design based on evidence.
