CASE STUDY 04 · LEARNING GOVERNANCE & QUALITY

Turning Quality Principles Into a Usable Governance Model

How I helped develop Valencia College's Rubric for Online Competencies: a shared framework for quality course design, observable delivery practices, peer and dean review, and continuous improvement of the online student learning experience.

Quality governance works best when it creates a common language for improvement without eliminating innovation or professional judgment. MY APPROACH TO LEARNING QUALITY
The Challenge
A quality framework needed to fit the institution, not just the industry

Valencia needed a practical way to define and review quality in online learning that reflected the college's own expectations, faculty competencies, student needs, and teaching context.

The goal was not simply to inspect course shells. The framework needed to connect the design of an online course with the instructor behaviors that bring that design to life, while supporting faculty development, peer and dean review, and continuous improvement.

How the Rubric Works

The rubric deliberately separates what is built into the course from what the instructor does during delivery. That distinction makes quality more observable and more actionable.

01

Design Criteria

Reviewers look for a clear presence of defined online-course components, such as orientation, navigation, accessibility, outcomes, assessments, technology, and support.

02

Delivery Criteria

Reviewers then examine observable instructor actions that show how the course design is actually supported in practice.

03

Annotations & Resources

Each criterion is paired with context, examples, resources, and links to Valencia's Essential Competencies for educators.

The Five Quality Areas

The rubric organizes online-learning quality into five major sections.

🚦

Start Right

Orientation, navigation, syllabus and schedule, course expectations, communication, technical requirements, academic integrity, support services, attendance verification, and community building.

♿

Accessibility & Usability

Clear organization, keyboard navigation, alternative formats, alt text, consistent design, captioning, transcripts, and accessible course materials.

🎯

Learning Outcomes, Activities & Materials

Measurable outcomes, alignment, relevant instructional materials, critical thinking, varied assessment, and activities that support achievement of course and module outcomes.

🖥

Course Technology & Support

Effective technology aligned to learning outcomes, active learning, working links, privacy information, and appropriate support for required tools.

💬

Engaged Teaching

Communication, feedback, gradebook use, formative assessment, module overviews, active-learning interaction, and clear expectations for participation and performance.

The work required balancing rigor with usability. The framework had to be specific enough to support consistent review, but developmental enough to help faculty improve rather than simply determine whether a course passed or failed.

Governance Without Eliminating Innovation
A feature I especially value in the model
The rubric intentionally created space to recognize outstanding online innovation and quality.

At the end of each major section, reviewers could identify course elements that created an especially strong student experience or used learning outcomes, technology, course materials, accessibility, or engaged teaching in an innovative way.

Shared standardsCore expectations make quality visible and discussable.
Professional judgmentThe model does not require every course to look or operate the same way.
Recognition of innovationReview explicitly makes room to identify practices that go beyond baseline expectations.
Developmental useFeedback can support redesign, coaching, and continued growth.

From Standards to Continuous Improvement

The rubric is most useful when it becomes part of an improvement cycle rather than a one-time compliance exercise.

01

Define

Create a shared institutional understanding of quality online design and delivery.

02

Review

Use peer and dean review to examine both course design and observable instructional practice.

03

Develop

Use findings, annotations, and resources to support faculty learning and course redesign.

04

Improve

Refine courses and practices over time while continuing to recognize effective innovation.
