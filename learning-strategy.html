From Learning Need to Sustainable Performance

I approach learning as an interconnected system rather than a one-time training event. The work begins with evidence and stakeholder needs, then moves through design, enablement, measurement, and iteration.

01
Discover

Use outcomes, observations, operational data, learner feedback, and stakeholder input to identify capability and performance gaps.

02
Architect

Translate needs into clear standards, learning pathways, reusable content, workflows, and role-appropriate supports.

03
Enable

Prepare the people who deliver and sustain learning through onboarding, mentoring, guidance, technology, and just-in-time resources.

04
Measure

Use success, retention, quality indicators, adoption data, and recurring implementation barriers to evaluate whether the system is working.

05
Improve

Turn evidence into targeted redesign, clearer expectations, better resources, and stronger systems rather than treating evaluation as the endpoint.

Why This Work Matters to Me

Access has always been more than a healthcare concept

I grew up in small rural Nebraska communities where access to healthcare depended on a limited number of hospitals and professionals serving large geographic areas. My father was a hospital administrator in those communities, often at the only hospital in the county, and I saw firsthand how difficult it could be to recruit physicians and other qualified professionals to rural areas.

I also saw dietitians and other healthcare professionals travel across multiple hospitals, nursing homes, and counties because the workforce simply was not available locally. Near the end of his career, my father helped develop a health center to expand access and meet community need.

Those early experiences shaped the way I understand workforce capacity, community health, and access. My own career later brought those themes together through clinical nutrition, health-sciences education, faculty and workforce development, and doctoral research focused on food insecurity and barriers to student success.

Strong systems matter because access depends on people being prepared, supported, and able to serve the communities that need them. THE THROUGH-LINE IN MY WORK
